1. Introduction

In the era of 3D integration and advanced packaging, Hybrid Cu Bonding has emerged as a critical technology for high-density interconnects. To evaluate the reliability and performance of these bonds, precisely engineered specimens are required. This post documents the end-to-end fabrication process of Copper (Cu) pad structures designed specifically for Chemical Mechanical Polishing (CMP) and subsequent bonding experiments.

The fabrication workflow follows a rigorous sequence:

  1. Mask Design
  2. PR Selection
  3. Lithography Optimization
  4. RIE Etching Optimization
  5. Seed Sputtering
  6. Electroplating

2. Objective

The core objective of this project was to optimize Photolithography and RIE Etching to achieve a near-vertical (90 degree) sidewall profile in the SiO2 layer.

3. Experimental Results & Workflow

3.1 Mask Design and Wafer Preparation

The mask was designed with multiple test groups to analyze the impact of spacing on bonding quality.

  • Patterns: Featured both 3um and 4um pads.
  • Variables: Each pattern size was further divided into two types based on spacing.
  • Substrate: 8-inch Si wafers with a 1.2um thick SiO2 layer grown via thermal oxidation.

3.2 Materials and Equipment

To achieve the required precision, industry-standard equipment and high-resolution photoresist (PR) were utilized:

  • Photoresist: AZ GXR 46cp (Spin-coated to a thickness of 1.8um).
  • Lithography: EV Group (EVG) 610 Aligner.
  • Dry Etching: Oxford Instruments PlasmaPro 80 Cobra65 (RIE).
  • Deposition: SRN-110 (Sputter) for the seed layer.
  • Metallization: TKC Electroplating system.

3.3 Process Optimization

A. Lithography: Overcoming Diffraction

Using i-line lithography in contact mode presented a challenge: controlling the sidewall slope. Diffraction at the mask-wafer interface can degrade pattern fidelity.

  • Strategy: We conducted a matrix of experiments varying Exposure Dose, Soft-Bake Temperature, and PR Thickness.
  • Focus: Finding the "sweet spot" where the slope was minimized to ensure the subsequent etching process remained highly anisotropic.

B. RIE Etching: Achieving Verticality

Once the Litho recipe was stabilized, the SiO2 layer was etched using Reactive Ion Etching (RIE).

  • Parameters: We iteratively adjusted Chamber Pressure, Gas Flow Rates, RF Power, and Gas Chemistry.
  • Goal: To achieve a near-90° vertical sidewall profile, which is essential for uniform Cu filling.
  • Evaluation: Results were verified using Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) to measure the etch depth and slope angle.

C. Metallization: Sputter & Electroplating

Following PR stripping, a Cu seed layer was deposited via sputtering (SRN-110). Finally, we utilized the TKC electroplating system to grow 2um of Cu. This thickness was specifically chosen to ensure enough material remains after the CMP process to achieve a perfectly planarized surface for hybrid bonding.

Reflections : Engineering Insights & Lessons Learned

Holistic Process Integration

Beyond the basic lithography principles learned in class, I realized that variables like PR type, thickness, Soft-bake, and PEB are not independent but deeply intercorrelated. Achieving a high-resolution profile required a holistic recalibration of the entire sequence, rather than adjusting isolated parameters.

Compensating for Limits via RIE

The etching process provided a critical opportunity to supplement lithography. By precisely tuning RF power and gas ratios, I learned to compensate for initial PR slope limitations, eventually achieving the target 90° verticality through a clear understanding of process trade-offs.

Bridging Theory and Reality

While literature and theory provided a roadmap, the actual fabrication often faced hardware-specific constraints. This project taught me to remain agile—to recognize the limits of theoretical models quickly and to pivot by identifying overlooked variables to reach engineering goals.
